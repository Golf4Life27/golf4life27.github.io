How a 1031 exchange calculation works

A 1031 exchange (a like-kind exchange under Section 1031 of the tax code) lets you sell investment real estate and buy other investment real estate without paying tax on the gain yet. The gain isn't erased: it's carried into the new property through a lower basis. Here's what the calculator works out:

  1. Realized gain = sale price − selling costs − adjusted basis, where adjusted basis is what you paid plus improvements minus the depreciation you've taken.
  2. Boot is anything you get out of the exchange that isn't real estate. Cash boot is sale proceeds you don't put into the new property. Mortgage boot is debt you got rid of and didn't replace: paying off a $200,000 loan and taking a $150,000 one is $50,000 of mortgage boot, unless you add $50,000 of your own cash.
  3. Taxable gain this year is the smaller of your realized gain and your total boot. The rest is deferred.
  4. Basis of the replacement property = its price − the deferred gain. That lower basis means less depreciation going forward and a bigger gain if you ever sell it outright.

The simple rule for deferring all of it: buy a replacement that costs at least what you sold for after costs, and put all the cash proceeds into it. Any debt you pay off has to be replaced with new debt or your own cash.

Buy a $500,000 property with a $100,000 loan instead, and you'd add $36,000 of cash but still have $64,000 of mortgage boot (the $200,000 loan paid off minus the $100,000 new loan and the $36,000 added), so $64,000 of gain is taxed and $280,000 is deferred. Buy $800,000 with a $400,000 loan and you add $36,000, there's no boot, and the whole $344,000 is deferred with a $456,000 basis.

The 45-day and 180-day deadlines

From the day the sale closes, you have 45 days to identify replacement property in writing (usually up to three properties, or more under the 200% or 95% rules) and 180 days to close on it. The 180 days are cut short by your tax return due date for the year of the sale, so a sale late in the year usually needs an extension on that return to keep the full window. Weekends and holidays don't extend either deadline. The sale proceeds have to go to a qualified intermediary, not to you; touching the cash generally ends the exchange.

What this calculator doesn't cover (general info, not tax advice)

It treats the boot as taxed as depreciation recapture first, which is the conservative way to estimate it, and leaves out the 3.8% net investment income tax, state-specific exchange rules, related-party rules, partial-year and vacation-home use, and reverse or improvement exchanges. Repair credits or other items paid through escrow can also count as boot. Tax rates are inputs because they depend on your income and state. FAQ

How do you calculate the gain deferred in a 1031 exchange?

Work out the realized gain (sale price minus selling costs minus your adjusted basis), then the boot (cash you keep plus debt you shed and don't replace). The taxable part is the smaller of the gain and the boot, and the rest is deferred. In the example, $344,000 of gain with $4,000 of cash boot defers $340,000.

What is boot in a 1031 exchange?

Boot is anything you receive that isn't like-kind real estate. Cash boot is sale proceeds you don't reinvest; mortgage boot is a drop in debt, such as paying off a $200,000 loan and taking a $150,000 one, unless you make up the difference with your own cash. Boot is taxed up to the amount of your gain.

How much do I have to reinvest to avoid all tax in a 1031 exchange?

Buy replacement property priced at least at your net sale price (sale price minus selling costs) and reinvest all of the cash proceeds. Debt you paid off must be replaced with new debt or added cash. Do both and there's no boot.

What is the basis of the replacement property after a 1031 exchange?

Generally the replacement's price minus the gain you deferred. A $700,000 property bought with $340,000 of deferred gain has a $360,000 basis, so future depreciation and the gain on a later sale are figured from that lower number.

Does a 1031 exchange defer depreciation recapture?

Yes. The depreciation you took is part of the deferred gain and carries into the replacement property. If boot is received, the taxable part can be taxed as recapture at up to 25%, which is how this calculator estimates it.

What are the 1031 exchange deadlines?

You must identify replacement property within 45 days after the sale closes and receive it within 180 days, or by your tax return due date (with extensions) for the year of the sale if that's earlier. For a sale on November 2, 2026, day 45 is December 17, 2026 and day 180 is May 1, 2027, so you'd need to extend your 2026 return to keep the full window.
