Airbnb Host Bookkeeping: Track Income, Expenses and Occupancy in One Place

Long-term landlords usually have one rent payment a month. Short-term rental hosts have a dozen payouts, cleaning fees, platform fees and supply runs — and the numbers that matter (occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, net after cleaning) live in three different apps.

If you host on Airbnb, Vrbo or both and still reconcile from CSV exports at tax time, this guide is the system that replaces the scramble. It works with any spreadsheet. We'll describe the layout KeyLedger uses in the Airbnb Host Spreadsheet so you can build it yourself or use a ready template.

General record-keeping guidance for short-term rental hosts, not tax advice. Platform 1099-K rules, lodging tax and Schedule E vs. Schedule C treatment vary by location and how you use the property. Confirm with a qualified tax professional.

Why a dedicated Airbnb host spreadsheet beats a bank feed alone

Bank and Stripe/Airbnb payout feeds show cash in. They do not show:

  • Booked nights vs. available nights (occupancy)
  • ADR (average daily rate) and RevPAR (revenue per available night)
  • Cleaning fees collected vs. cleaning costs paid
  • Platform service fees as a separate cost of booking
  • Which listing earned what when you have more than one

Those are the numbers hosts use to raise rates, cut dead months and decide whether a co-host is worth the fee. A short-term rental tracker that starts from bookings, not bank rows, makes them automatic.

The five tabs that cover 95% of host bookkeeping

1. Settings (listings + categories)

List every active listing once:

Field Why it matters
Nickname Same spelling everywhere (dropdowns)
Address / unit For your records and your preparer
Bedrooms Optional; useful for ADR comparisons
Primary channel Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com, Direct
Active? Yes/No so offline listings don't inflate available nights

Keep a fixed category list for income and expenses so January and December use the same words. Common short-term rental categories:

Income: Accommodation payout · Cleaning fees collected · Other guest fees · Resolution / adjustment credit

Expenses: Platform service fee · Cleaning & turnover · Supplies & toiletries · Laundry · Utilities · Internet · Insurance · Property taxes · Mortgage interest · HOA · Repairs · Co-host / management · Advertising · Software · Permits & lodging tax paid · Capital improvement (tracked, not expensed)

2. Bookings (one row per reservation)

This is the heart of an Airbnb income and expense tracker. Columns that pull their weight:

  • Confirmation #
  • Listing (dropdown)
  • Guest name
  • Channel
  • Check-in / check-out → nights = check-out − check-in
  • Guests
  • Booking total
  • Cleaning fee
  • Platform fee
  • Host payout
  • Status (Confirmed / Checked out / Cancelled)

Exclude cancelled stays from occupancy and revenue. If a guest cancelled after a partial payout, log the kept amount under Other guest fees or as a separate note — ask your CPA how your state treats it.

3. Expenses (one row per cost)

Same idea as a landlord expense log, but with STR categories. Tag every row to a listing so you can see whether Oak Cottage's cleaning bill is eating the payout while Lakeview still nets clean.

4. Monthly summary (filter by listing)

For each month, calculate:

Booked nights
Available nights  = active listings × days in month
Occupancy %       = booked ÷ available
Gross booking $
Cleaning fees $
Platform fees $
Host payout $
Expenses $
Net               = payout − expenses
ADR $             = payout ÷ booked nights
RevPAR $          = payout ÷ available nights

Filter to one listing when you want to know which unit is carrying the portfolio.

5. Dashboard (year at a glance)

Year-to-date gross booking, host payout, expenses, net, occupancy, ADR and RevPAR, plus a simple payout-vs-expenses chart. Ten minutes a week of logging keeps this current; tax season becomes an export, not an archaeology dig.

A worked mini-example

One listing, March:
Available nights 31
Booked nights 22
Occupancy 71%
Host payout $3,080
Cleaning + supplies + utilities $640
Net $2,440
ADR $140
RevPAR $99

If April payout is similar but booked nights drop to 14, ADR may look "better" while RevPAR and net fall. Hosts who only watch ADR miss that. Occupancy + RevPAR + net together tell the truth.

Tax and lodging-tax notes (high level)

  • Platform 1099-K / annual summaries are starting points, not full books. Fees, refunds and off-platform direct bookings often need reconciling.
  • Lodging / occupancy tax remitted to a city or state is usually tracked as its own expense (or a liability), not mixed into "utilities."
  • Schedule E vs. Schedule C (and average-period-of-rental rules) depends on how you use the property. This system organizes numbers either way; your preparer chooses the form.
  • Capital improvements (new HVAC, full remodel) should stay off the regular expense total so they are not deducted as if they were towels and toilet paper.

None of the above is advice for your return — it is the reason a clean vacation rental bookkeeping log saves preparer hours.

Checklist: set this up in one evening

  1. ☐ Create Settings with every listing marked Active = Yes
  2. ☐ Paste or type the last 90 days of bookings (or start from today and go forward)
  3. ☐ Log cleaning, supplies and utilities for the same period
  4. ☐ Confirm cancelled stays are marked Cancelled
  5. ☐ Open the monthly summary and sanity-check occupancy for last month
  6. ☐ Save a dated copy before you change categories mid-year
