How to calculate occupancy rate for an Airbnb

Occupancy rate = nights booked ÷ nights available. If you left 30 nights open and 20 were booked, occupancy is 20 ÷ 30 = 66.7%. The part people disagree on is the bottom number. Nights you blocked for yourself, family or repairs aren't available to guests, and many market-data tools leave them out, so pick one rule and use it every month. Otherwise a month with a lot of blocked nights makes a listing look busier than it is.

Occupancy alone doesn't say whether the listing makes money. A 70% month can lose money on a big mortgage, and a 45% month can profit on a paid-off place. What counts is the gap between your occupancy and your break-even occupancy, the share of nights you must book to cover every cost.

How break-even occupancy works

Each booked night brings in the nightly rate plus its share of the cleaning fee, less the platform fee and percent costs, less its share of the cleaning bill. What's left is that night's contribution toward fixed costs. Break-even nights = fixed costs ÷ contribution per night, and break-even occupancy is that divided by the nights available.

The listing runs 12.2 points above break-even. That cushion is about 3.7 nights a month; lose 4 bookings to a slow season and the month is in the red. That's the number to compare with how similar listings in your market book, month by month, rather than with a peak-summer screenshot.

Higher rate or higher occupancy?

Revenue per available night (RevPAN) is revenue divided by every night you could have sold, so it rewards rate and occupancy together. Try this in the calculator: raise the rate 10% to $165 and lose 2 booked nights (18 nights, 60% occupancy). Revenue becomes $3,510, RevPAN drops from $120 to $117, and profit slips from $471 to $454. A 10% price rise that costs you 10% of your nights is roughly a wash, but it also means fewer turnovers and less wear, so it can still be worth it. If the higher rate loses more nights than that, you're worse off.

Ways to lower break-even occupancy

  • Raise the contribution per night: a slightly higher rate, a cleaning fee that actually covers the cleaner, or a longer average stay (fewer turnovers per booked night) all lift it.
  • Cut fixed costs: re-quote insurance, switch utility plans and drop software you don't use. Fixed costs sit at the top of the break-even formula, so every dollar saved lowers the nights you need.
  • Check the fee setting: with the split fee hosts usually pay about 3%; with the host-only fee about 15.5%. Change the platform fee box to see how much break-even occupancy moves.

FAQ

How do I calculate occupancy rate for an Airbnb?

Divide the nights booked by the nights available and multiply by 100. If 20 of 30 available nights were booked, occupancy is 66.7%. Decide up front whether blocked nights count as available and keep that rule the same every month.

What is break-even occupancy for a short-term rental?

It's the share of available nights you must book to cover all your costs with zero profit. The calculator takes fixed monthly costs and divides them by what each booked night contributes after the platform fee, percent costs and cleaning, then divides by the nights available.

What is a good occupancy rate for an Airbnb?

It depends on your market, season and costs, so there's no single number. A good occupancy rate is one comfortably above your own break-even occupancy and in line with similar listings nearby. Use the margin of safety line in the calculator to see how much room you have.

What is RevPAN?

Revenue per available night is booking revenue divided by all nights you left open, booked or not. It combines occupancy and nightly rate in one number, so you can compare a high-rate, low-occupancy strategy with a lower-rate, higher-occupancy one.

How is break-even nightly rate different from break-even occupancy?

Break-even occupancy asks how many nights you must book at your current rate. Break-even nightly rate asks what rate you need to charge to cover costs if the number of booked nights stays as entered. The calculator shows both.

Does the cleaning fee count as revenue?

Yes, in this calculator the cleaning fee guests pay is revenue and the cleaner and supplies you pay are a cost per stay. If your fee exactly covers the cost, cleaning has no effect on profit; if the fee is lower, each turnover quietly reduces your margin.
