How the Airbnb profit calculation works

An average month has 30.4 nights (365 ÷ 12). Multiply by occupancy to get booked nights, and divide by the average stay to get the number of turnovers. Then:

  1. Booking revenue = booked nights × nightly rate + turnovers × cleaning fee. Guest service fees and occupancy taxes are left out because they never belong to you.
  2. Payout = booking revenue − Airbnb's host fee.
  3. Per-stay costs = turnovers × (cleaning cost + supplies), plus a co-host percentage of the payout and a repair reserve as a percentage of revenue.
  4. Net profit = payout − per-stay costs − fixed monthly costs (mortgage, tax, insurance, utilities, HOA, software and permits).
  5. Break-even occupancy = fixed costs ÷ (30.4 × what each booked night contributes after the fee, cleanings, supplies and reserve).

The calendar looks busy, yet the margin over break-even is only about two percentage points. Two slow weeks and the month is underwater. That's the number to check before you buy, furnish or renew a lease for arbitrage: compare break-even occupancy with what similar listings in your market actually book, season by season, not with a peak-summer screenshot.

How to improve a thin result

  • Fee structure: on the split-fee setting hosts usually pay about 3% and guests pay the rest. Re-run the calculator with 3 in the fee box to see the difference, then decide how to price.
  • Minimum stay: longer stays mean fewer turnovers. Try a 4 or 5 night average and watch cleaning and supply costs drop.
  • Cleaning fee vs. cost: if you charge less than the cleaner costs, every booking loses money on the turnover before the rent even counts.
  • Fixed costs: insurance, utilities and software are easy to underestimate. Pull 12 months of real bills.

FAQ

How do I calculate Airbnb profit?

Take booked nights × nightly rate plus cleaning fees, subtract Airbnb's host fee to get your payout, then subtract cleaning and supply costs per stay, any co-host fee, a repair reserve and your fixed monthly costs (mortgage or rent, property tax, insurance, utilities, HOA, software, permits). What's left is net profit.

What is break-even occupancy for an Airbnb?

It's the share of nights you need booked to cover every cost with zero profit. The calculator divides fixed monthly costs by what each booked night contributes after the host fee, cleaning, supplies and reserve. If your market's typical occupancy is close to or below it, the listing is risky.

What fee does Airbnb take from hosts?

It depends on the fee setting. With the split fee, hosts usually pay about 3% and guests pay a separate service fee. With the host-only fee, which is standard for hosts who connect through property management software, the host pays about 15.5% and guests pay no service fee. Your Airbnb earnings page shows which applies to you.

Should occupancy or lodging taxes be included?

No. In many places Airbnb collects and remits them for you, and where you remit them yourself the guest still pays them. Either way they pass through and aren't your revenue, so leave them out of the nightly rate.

What is a good occupancy rate for an Airbnb?

There isn't one universal number. A good occupancy is one comfortably above your own break-even, in your slow season as well as your busy one. Use the break-even figure here and compare it with booking data for similar listings nearby.

Is cash-on-cash return the same as ROI?

Not quite. Cash-on-cash is one year of net cash flow divided by the cash you put in (down payment, closing costs and furnishing). It ignores appreciation and loan paydown, which a full ROI would include, so it's the stricter test of whether the rental pays you today.
