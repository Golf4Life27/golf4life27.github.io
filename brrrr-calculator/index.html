How a BRRRR deal works

BRRRR stands for Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. You buy a house that needs work, usually with short-term money, fix it up, rent it out, then refinance into a long-term mortgage based on the new appraised value. The refinance pays off the short-term loan and, if the numbers work, hands your own cash back so you can do the next one.

The number that decides whether you can "repeat" is cash left in the deal: everything the project cost, minus the short-term loan, minus the cash that comes back at the refinance. Interest, points, months of holding costs and refinance closing costs are the items people forget, and they're exactly what leaves money stuck in the property.

Worked example: a $240,000 ARV BRRRR

You buy for $140,000 plus $4,000 of closing costs, with a short-term loan of 80% of the price ($112,000) at 11% interest-only and 2 points. The rehab is $45,000, it takes six months to refinance, and holding costs run $450 a month. It appraises at $240,000 and you refinance at 75% LTV into a 30-year loan at 6.9% with $5,000 of closing costs. It rents for $2,150 a month.

Interest: $112,000 × 11% × 6/12, plus 2 points$8,400
Holding: $450 × 6 months$2,700
Total project cost: $140,000 + $4,000 + $45,000 + $8,400 + $2,700$200,100
Your cash in: $200,100 − $112,000 short-term loan$88,100
New loan: 75% of $240,000$180,000
Cash out: $180,000 − $112,000 payoff − $5,000 closing$63,000
Cash left in the deal: $88,100 − $63,000$25,100
Cash flow after the refinance: $1,347.43 NOI − $1,185.48 P&I$161.95 a month

You end up controlling a $240,000 rental with $60,000 of equity and $25,100 of your own money still in it. That's a 7.7% cash-on-cash return on the cash left in, with a DSCR of 1.14. To get every dollar back on these terms you'd have to pay about $116,321 or less. That ceiling is a little higher than simply subtracting $25,100 from the price, because a lower price also means a smaller short-term loan with less interest and fewer points.

The levers that matter most

  • The appraisal: at 75% LTV, every $10,000 of appraised value returns $7,500 more cash. Base the ARV on recent sold comps of similar renovated homes.
  • Rehab overruns: every dollar over budget is a dollar left in the deal unless the appraisal rises with it.
  • Time: each extra month adds interest and holding costs, and many lenders require a seasoning period before a cash-out refinance based on the new value.
  • The new rate: a higher rate doesn't change cash out, but it raises the payment and can turn the cash flow negative. Try 7.5% or 8% in the calculator.
  • DSCR lenders: many investors refinance with a DSCR loan, which often needs a ratio around 1.20 to 1.25. Check yours with the DSCR calculator.

BRRRR vs. a flip

A flip sells the house after the rehab and takes the profit as cash, paying selling costs and usually ordinary income tax on the gain. BRRRR keeps the house, pulls cash out with a loan instead of a sale, and leaves you with a rental and its cash flow. If the deal doesn't cash flow on the new loan, compare it as a flip with the fix and flip calculator. FAQ

How do you calculate cash left in a BRRRR deal?

Add up the total project cost (price, closing costs, rehab, short-term interest and points, holding costs), subtract the short-term loan to get your cash in, then subtract the cash that comes back at the refinance (new loan minus the short-term payoff minus refinance closing costs). In the example, $88,100 in minus $63,000 out leaves $25,100 in the deal.

How much can you cash out on a BRRRR refinance?

The new loan is usually the appraised value times the lender's loan-to-value limit, often 70% to 75% for an investment property cash-out refinance. From that, the short-term loan is paid off and closing costs come out; what's left comes to you.

What is the max price for a BRRRR deal?

The highest purchase price where cash left in the deal is zero. The calculator solves for it with your ARV, LTV, rehab, timeline and loan terms, and it accounts for the short-term loan getting smaller as the price drops.

What if the cash-on-cash return says infinite?

That means the refinance returned all of your cash, so there is no cash left in to divide by. Any positive cash flow is then a return on zero dollars of your own money.

Is a BRRRR deal good if cash is left in?

It can be. Leaving some cash in still gets you a rental and equity; you just can't repeat the next deal with the same money. Compare the cash-on-cash return on the cash left in with your other options.

Does the calculator include seasoning or lender rules?

No. Lenders set their own LTV limits, seasoning periods and DSCR minimums, and the appraisal may come in lower than your ARV. Use conservative numbers and check with your lender.
