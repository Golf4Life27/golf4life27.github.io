The cap rate formula

Capitalization rate = net operating income ÷ property value (or price). Flip it around and you get value: value = NOI ÷ cap rate. A 6% cap rate on a $500,000 property means $30,000 a year of NOI; a property with $30,000 of NOI is worth $500,000 to a buyer who wants 6%.

Net operating income is the rent you actually collect plus other income, minus the costs of running the property:

  • Include: property taxes, insurance, management, repairs and maintenance, a capital expenditure reserve, owner-paid utilities, HOA dues, landscaping, and an allowance for vacancy and unpaid rent.
  • Leave out: mortgage principal and interest, depreciation, and income taxes. Cap rate describes the property, not how you finance it.

Worked example: a $250,000 rental

A duplex is listed at $250,000. The two units rent for $2,500 a month combined, and you use 5% for vacancy and 8% for management.

Cap rate is not your return

Cap rate assumes you paid all cash. Once there's a loan, your actual return depends on the rate and the down payment. Take a 6% cap on $500,000 ($30,000 NOI) with 75% financing interest-only at 7%: interest is $26,250, leaving $3,750 on $125,000 down, a 3% cash-on-cash return. The cap rate is the same 6% either way. Use cap rate to compare properties and price them against the market, then run cash flow and cash-on-cash with your real loan terms before you offer.

What is a good cap rate?

There's no single number. Cap rates move with the market, interest rates, the property type and the neighborhood: newer buildings in strong areas trade at lower cap rates, and older properties or weaker areas need higher ones to attract buyers. Compare against recent sales of similar properties nearby, and be skeptical of a seller's "pro forma" NOI. Build NOI from actual trailing-12-month numbers with realistic vacancy and repair costs, because an inflated NOI makes any cap rate look good.

FAQ

How do you calculate cap rate?

Divide the property's yearly net operating income by its price or value. A property with $18,120 of NOI listed at $250,000 has a cap rate of $18,120 ÷ $250,000 = 7.25%.

What is included in NOI for cap rate?

Collected rent and other income minus operating costs: taxes, insurance, management, repairs, a capex reserve, owner-paid utilities and HOA dues, with an allowance for vacancy. Mortgage payments, depreciation and income taxes are not included.

Does cap rate include the mortgage?

No. Cap rate is measured before any debt, as if you paid cash. To see the effect of a loan, look at cash flow after the mortgage and cash-on-cash return.

How do I find a property's value from a cap rate?

Divide the NOI by the cap rate as a decimal. $18,120 of NOI at a 7% cap rate is $18,120 ÷ 0.07 = $258,857.

What is a good cap rate for a rental property?

It depends on the market, property type and interest rates. Lower cap rates usually mean lower risk and higher prices; higher cap rates mean more income per dollar but often more risk. Compare with recent sales of similar properties in the same area.

Is a higher cap rate better?

A higher cap rate means more NOI for each dollar of price, which is better for a buyer if the income is real. It can also signal an older building, a weaker location or income that's less certain, so check what's behind it.
