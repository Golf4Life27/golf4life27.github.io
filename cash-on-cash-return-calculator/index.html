The cash-on-cash return formula

Cash-on-cash return = yearly pre-tax cash flow ÷ total cash invested. Cash flow is the rent you collect minus operating expenses and mortgage payments. Cash invested is everything that came out of your pocket to buy the property: the down payment, closing costs, and any rehab you paid in cash.

It answers a simple question: for every dollar I put in, how many cents come back each year? A deal that takes $80,000 of your cash and throws off $6,400 a year after all costs is an 8% cash-on-cash return. Need $10,000 more to close and the same house drops to 7.1%, every year you own it.

Worked example: a $200,000 rental with 25% down

You buy a single-family rental for $200,000 with 25% down and a 30-year loan at 7%. Closing costs are $6,000 and you put $10,000 into repairs. It rents for $2,000 a month; you budget 5% vacancy and 8% management.

Loan: $150,000 at 7% for 30 years$997.95 a month
Collected rent: $24,000 less 5% vacancy$22,800
Expenses: taxes $2,400 + insurance $1,200 + management $1,824 + maintenance and capex $2,400 + other $600$8,424
Net operating income$14,376
Less mortgage payments: $997.95 × 12$11,975
Cash flow per year$2,401
Cash-on-cash return: $2,401 ÷ $66,0003.64%

The property itself is a 7.19% cap rate, but at a 7% interest rate the loan eats most of the income, so your cash earns 3.64%. In year one the loan balance also drops by about $1,524, which lifts the return to about 5.95% if you count principal paydown. To get an 8% cash-on-cash return with the same terms, rent and costs, you would need to buy at about $163,952 or less.

Cash-on-cash vs. cap rate

Cap rate (NOI ÷ price) describes the property as if you paid all cash, so it's the way to compare buildings and check a price against the market. Cash-on-cash return includes your loan, so it describes your deal. When the interest rate is higher than the cap rate, borrowing pulls your cash-on-cash return below the cap rate; when it's lower, leverage pushes it above. Check both. The cap rate calculator covers the NOI side in more detail, and if you're financing with a DSCR loan, the DSCR calculator checks whether the rent covers the payment and shows the largest loan the rent supports.

What is a good cash-on-cash return?

There's no official number. Many rental investors look for something in the high single digits or better, and compare it with what the same cash could earn elsewhere with less work and risk. What counts as good depends on your market, the condition of the property, and how much appreciation and principal paydown you expect on top. Whatever target you use, build the cash flow from realistic rent, vacancy, repairs and capital reserves; an optimistic expense line makes any return look good.

What cash-on-cash return leaves out

  • Principal paydown and appreciation: they build wealth but aren't cash in hand. The calculator shows year-one paydown separately.
  • Taxes: it's a pre-tax number, before depreciation and income tax.
  • Time: it's one year's snapshot. Rents, costs and refinancing change it later.

FAQ

How do you calculate cash-on-cash return?

Divide the yearly pre-tax cash flow by the total cash you invested. With $2,401 of cash flow on $66,000 invested (down payment, closing costs and rehab), the cash-on-cash return is $2,401 ÷ $66,000 = 3.64%.

What counts as cash invested?

Every dollar you paid out of pocket to acquire the property: the down payment, closing costs, loan fees, and repairs or rehab paid in cash. Money you borrowed is not cash invested.

Is cash-on-cash return the same as ROI?

It's one kind of return on investment. Cash-on-cash only counts the cash the property pays you in a year. Broader ROI measures can add principal paydown, appreciation and tax benefits.

What is the difference between cash-on-cash return and cap rate?

Cap rate is NOI divided by the price and ignores financing. Cash-on-cash return is cash flow after mortgage payments divided by the cash you put in, so it changes with your down payment and interest rate.

What is a good cash-on-cash return for a rental property?

It depends on the market, the property and your goals. Many investors want a return in the high single digits or more, but compare it with your other options and make sure the expense estimates are realistic.

How do I find the most I can pay to hit my target return?

Enter your target in the calculator. It solves for the price where cash flow divided by cash invested equals the target, keeping your down payment percentage, interest rate, rent and costs the same.
