The DSCR formula

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) measures how many times the property's income covers its loan payment. There are two common versions, and it matters which one you're being asked for:

  • DSCR loans on 1-4 unit rentals: DSCR = gross monthly rent ÷ PITIA (principal, interest, property taxes, insurance and HOA dues). On an interest-only loan the payment is ITIA. The rent is usually the lower of the lease and the appraiser's market rent.
  • Commercial and portfolio analysis: DSCR = net operating income ÷ annual debt service. NOI is rent after vacancy and every operating expense, so this version is stricter.

A DSCR of 1.00 means the income exactly covers the payment. At 1.25 the income is 125% of the payment, a 25% cushion. Requirements vary by lender and program; many DSCR loan programs ask for somewhere between 1.00 and 1.25, and a higher ratio often gets better pricing or a higher loan-to-value.

How to raise your DSCR

  • Borrow less: a bigger down payment cuts the payment directly. The calculator's max loan shows exactly how far.
  • Support a higher rent: a signed lease at market rent, or a stronger market rent on the appraisal.
  • Lower the T, I and A: shop insurance, and check the tax estimate the lender is using (purchases can be reassessed).
  • Interest-only or a longer term: a lower payment raises the ratio, but you pay down less principal.

What DSCR leaves out

A lender-style DSCR uses gross rent, so it ignores vacancy, management, repairs and capital expenses. A property can qualify at 1.25 and still lose money every month. FAQ

How do you calculate DSCR for a rental property?

For a DSCR loan, divide the gross monthly rent by the monthly PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and HOA). $2,400 rent ÷ $1,780.60 PITIA = 1.35. For commercial-style analysis, divide yearly net operating income by yearly loan payments instead.

What is a good DSCR?

1.00 means the rent just covers the payment. Many DSCR loan programs ask for 1.00 to 1.25, and some allow lower ratios with a smaller loan or higher rate. For your own safety margin, also check the NOI-based ratio after vacancy and repairs.

How much rent do I need for a 1.25 DSCR?

Multiply the monthly PITIA by 1.25. With a $1,780.60 PITIA you need about $2,226 of monthly rent. Put another way, the PITIA can be at most 80% of the rent.

How do I find the max loan amount for a DSCR loan?

Divide the rent by the target DSCR to get the maximum PITIA, subtract taxes, insurance and HOA to get the maximum principal and interest, then find the loan that payment supports at your rate and term. The calculator does this automatically, for amortizing and interest-only loans.

Does an interest-only loan change the DSCR?

Yes. Many DSCR programs qualify interest-only loans on the ITIA payment (interest, taxes, insurance and HOA), which is lower than PITIA, so the ratio is higher. Choose interest-only in the calculator to see the difference.

Is DSCR the same as cash flow?

No. The lender ratio uses gross rent and ignores vacancy, management, repairs and capital expenses. A deal can meet a 1.25 DSCR and still have thin or negative cash flow, which is why the calculator also shows NOI, the NOI-based DSCR and monthly cash flow.
