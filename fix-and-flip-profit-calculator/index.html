How to calculate fix and flip profit

Flip profit = sale price minus everything the project cost: the purchase price, closing costs on the buy, the rehab (with a cushion for surprises), loan interest and points, the monthly costs of holding the house, and the cost of selling it. The common mistake is stopping at "ARV minus price minus rehab". Financing, holding and selling costs together can eat a large share of that spread.

The fix is to start from the exit and work backward: decide the profit you need first, then subtract it along with every cost from the ARV. What's left is the most you can pay. The calculator does that solve for you.

Worked example: a $340,000 ARV flip

You buy a house for $200,000 with 2% closing costs, budget $50,000 for the rehab plus a 10% contingency, and expect it to sell for $340,000 after repairs. A hard money lender funds 85% of price plus rehab at 11% interest-only with 2 points. The project takes six months, holding costs run $800 a month, and selling costs are 8% of the sale price.

Rehab: $50,000 + 10% contingency$55,000
Loan: 85% of $255,000 = $216,750; interest at 11% for 6 months$11,921
Points: 2% of the loan$4,335
Holding: $800 × 6 months$4,800
Selling costs: 8% of $340,000$27,200
Total project cost$307,256
Net profit: $340,000 − $307,256$32,744
Return on your $63,306 of cash51.7%

The simple spread (ARV minus price minus rehab) was $85,000, but the real profit is $32,744, or 9.6% of the sale price. If you need $40,000 for the risk and the months of work, the most you can offer on these terms is about $193,304. The house has to sell for about $304,409 just to break even, so missing the ARV by about 10% wipes out the profit. The 70% rule (70% of ARV minus repairs) gives $183,000 here, a more conservative offer.

Why flips lose money

  • Holding time runs long: every extra month adds interest, taxes, insurance and utilities. Change months from 6 to 9 and watch the profit.
  • Rehab overruns: that's what the contingency is for. Many flippers use 10% to 20% depending on the house's age and condition.
  • ARV is too optimistic: base it on recent sold comps of similar renovated homes, not list prices. Compare the break-even price with your ARV to see how much room you have.
  • Forgotten exit costs: agent commissions, seller closing costs, and credits to the buyer after inspection all come out of the sale.

Fix and flip calculator vs. the 70% rule

The 70% rule is a fast screen: offer no more than 70% of ARV minus repairs. It bakes in a rough allowance for profit, holding and selling costs, so it's handy when you're sorting through many leads. This calculator replaces that rough allowance with your actual loan terms, timeline and costs, which matters most on expensive houses (where 30% of ARV is a big cushion) and cheap ones (where it may not be enough). FAQ

How do you calculate profit on a house flip?

Take the sale price and subtract the purchase price, closing costs on the purchase, the rehab and contingency, loan interest and points, holding costs for every month you own it, and selling costs. In the example, $340,000 minus $307,256 of total costs leaves $32,744 before income tax.

What is a good profit on a fix and flip?

There's no official number. Many flippers set a minimum dollar profit or a percentage of the sale price that's worth the risk and months of work. Set your own target in the calculator and it shows the most you can pay to reach it.

What are holding costs on a flip?

Costs you pay each month while you own the house: loan interest, property taxes, insurance, utilities, HOA dues, and lawn care or security. The calculator takes loan interest from the loan inputs and everything else from the monthly holding cost field.

How is the max offer calculated?

The calculator solves for the purchase price where net profit equals your target, keeping the ARV, rehab, timeline, loan terms and cost percentages the same. Because closing costs, the loan and its interest all grow with the price, it solves for them together instead of simply subtracting.

What is the 70% rule in house flipping?

A rule of thumb that says to pay no more than 70% of the after-repair value minus the repair cost. On a $340,000 ARV with $55,000 of repairs, that's $238,000 minus $55,000 = $183,000.

Does flip profit include taxes?

No. The calculator shows profit before income tax. Flip profits are often taxed as ordinary income, so check with a tax professional about your situation.
