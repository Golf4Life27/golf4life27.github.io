How to calculate prorated rent

Prorated rent is the monthly rent turned into a daily rate, times the number of days the tenant is responsible for. Count both the first and the last day: a move-in on the 15th of a 31-day month covers the 15th through the 31st, which is 17 days. The only real question is how the daily rate is figured, and leases use one of three methods:

  1. Actual days in the month: daily rate = monthly rent ÷ days in that month (28 to 31). This is the most common method and always charges exactly the full rent for a full month.
  2. 30-day month (sometimes called a banker's month): daily rate = monthly rent ÷ 30, the same in every month. It's simple, but it charges a bit more per day in 31-day months and a bit less in February, so the calculator caps it at one full month's rent.
  3. 365-day year: daily rate = monthly rent × 12 ÷ 365. Every day of the year costs the same. The calculator caps this one at a full month too.

Worked example: moving in on the 15th

Rent is $1,800 a month. The tenant gets the keys on October 15, and October has 31 days, so they owe for the 15th through the 31st, which is 17 days.

Actual days: $1,800 ÷ 31 = $58.06 a day × 17$987.10
30-day month: $1,800 ÷ 30 = $60.00 a day × 17$1,020.00
365-day year: $21,600 ÷ 365 = $59.18 a day × 17$1,006.03
Due at move-in (actual days + November's $1,800 + $1,800 deposit)$4,587.10

The methods are only about $33 apart here, but they're never zero apart in a 31-day month, which is why the lease should say which one applies. Watch the opposite mistake too: a tenant who pays the full $1,800 for October and gets $300 knocked off November has only been credited for about 5 days, when 14 days (the 1st through the 14th) went unused. Under the actual-days method that credit should be $812.90.

Moving out mid-month

Use 1 as the first day and the move-out date as the last day. A tenant leaving on the 10th of a 30-day month at $1,800 rent owes 10 days: $600 under the actual-days and 30-day methods, or $591.78 under the 365-day method, because in a 30-day month the first two are identical. Before prorating a move-out, check the lease: many leases require a full month's notice or don't allow proration at the end of the term, and local rules vary.

Keep it on the ledger

Record the prorated charge and the payment as their own lines on the tenant's ledger, with the dates and the method used, so there's no argument later about what was owed. The same goes for the deposit: note the amount and date received. At tax time, prorated rent is ordinary rental income for the month you receive it. FAQ

How do I calculate prorated rent?

Divide the monthly rent by the number of days in the month to get a daily rate, then multiply by the days the tenant is responsible for, counting both the first and last day. For $1,800 rent and a move-in on the 15th of a 31-day month, that's $1,800 ÷ 31 × 17 = $987.10.

Which prorated rent method is correct?

Whichever one your lease names. Actual days in the month is the most common. Some leases use a flat 30-day month or a 365-day year instead. If the lease is silent, landlord and tenant should agree on a method in writing before the payment is made.

Do I count the move-in day?

Yes, normally the day the tenant gets possession counts as a rent day. A move-in on the 15th of a 31-day month is 17 days: the 15th through the 31st.

Is a landlord required to prorate rent?

It depends on the lease and on state and local rules, which vary. Many landlords prorate the first month as standard practice. At move-out, many leases require full notice and don't prorate. Check your lease and local rules rather than assuming.

What's due at move-in with prorated rent?

Usually the prorated rent for the first partial month plus the security deposit. If the move-in is late in the month, many landlords also collect the next full month's rent up front so the tenant isn't due again a few days later. The calculator adds it in when you enter 1 in that box.

How do I prorate rent for February?

Use 28 days (29 in a leap year) with the actual-days method. A 30-day-month method charges less per day than the real February rate, and a 365-day method charges about the same per day as any other month, so the three methods differ most in February and 31-day months.
