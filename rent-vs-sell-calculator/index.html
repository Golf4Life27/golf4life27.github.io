How the rent vs sell comparison works

Selling now and renting the house out are compared over the same number of years, so the two totals are worth the same thing at the same date:

Sell now = home value − selling costs − mortgage payoff − any tax on the gain, then that cash grows at the return you'd earn investing it elsewhere. Keep and rent = the yearly rental cash flow (rent after vacancy, running costs and the mortgage, also invested at that return) + the cash from selling at the end, after selling costs, the smaller loan balance and tax. The yearly return on the equity you keep is the rate the cash you leave in the house grows at; if it beats the return you could get elsewhere, keeping tends to win.

Keeping comes out ahead by$69,732 (12.8% a year on the equity kept)

The cheap 3% loan does a lot of the work here: the rent covers the payment with room to spare, and about $36,000 of the loan gets paid off in 5 years. Change a few inputs and the answer flips. At 0% appreciation keeping is only about $18,800 ahead; lower the rent to $1,900 as well and the year-one cash flow turns negative, and selling now comes out about $14,500 ahead. With a 7% mortgage instead of 3%, keeping is about $17,000 ahead but you'd be putting in about $2,800 in the first year to cover the shortfall. The numbers also leave out the time and hassle of being a landlord, a bad tenant or a big repair, so many owners want keeping to win by a clear margin before they rent it out.

Taxes when you sell a former home (general info)

Tax is the input most likely to change the answer, and it depends on your situation, so the calculator just takes the rate you expect. As general information about US rules: owners who owned and lived in a home as their main home for at least 2 of the 5 years before the sale may be able to exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for married couples filing jointly). Renting the house out for more than about 3 years can mean you no longer meet that test when you sell, and depreciation claimed while it was a rental generally can't be excluded. That's why the example uses 0% tax for selling now and 15% for selling later. Check IRS Publication 523 or a tax professional before you decide; this page is not tax advice.

FAQ

Should I rent or sell my house?

Compare what each choice is worth after the same number of years. Selling now gives you cash you can invest; keeping it gives you rental cash flow, loan paydown and appreciation, but you pay selling costs later and possibly more tax. In the example, keeping a $400,000 house with a 3% mortgage for 5 years comes out about $69,700 ahead of selling now and investing at 5%, but lower rent and no appreciation flip it to selling.

Is it better to keep a house with a low mortgage rate and rent it out?

Often, if the rent covers the payment and running costs. A low rate means a small payment and faster principal paydown, so the equity you leave in the house can earn more than it would elsewhere. Check that the year-one cash flow is positive with realistic repairs, vacancy and management, and that you're comfortable being a landlord.

How much does it cost to sell a house?

Selling costs commonly run around 6% to 10% of the sale price once agent commissions, closing costs, transfer taxes and any repairs or concessions are added up. They apply whether you sell now or later, which is why a short rental period rarely pays.

What happens to the home sale tax exclusion if I rent my house out?

As general information for the US, the exclusion of up to $250,000 ($500,000 for married couples filing jointly) usually requires owning and living in the home as your main home for 2 of the 5 years before the sale. Renting it out for more than about 3 years can mean you lose it, and depreciation taken as a rental generally isn't covered. Check IRS Publication 523 or a tax professional for your situation.

What return should I use for investing the sale cash?

Use what you'd realistically earn on the money: for example a high-yield savings rate, a bond yield, or a long-run stock return if you'd invest it that way. The higher that return, the better selling looks, so try a few values.

What does this calculator leave out?

Income tax on the rent and the yearly depreciation deduction, depreciation recapture, landlord insurance changes, big one-off repairs, and your time. Treat the result as a pre-tax comparison of the money, then weigh the work and risk of being a landlord.
