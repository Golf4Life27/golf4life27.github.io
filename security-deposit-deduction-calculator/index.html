How security deposit deductions work

At move-out, the landlord can generally keep part of the deposit for money the tenant owes under the lease: unpaid rent and fees, cleaning needed to bring the unit back to its move-in condition, and repairs for damage beyond normal wear and tear. Everything left over goes back to the tenant, usually with a written, itemized list of each deduction. The math is simple:

  1. Start with the deposit, plus any interest your state, city or lease requires you to pay on it.
  2. Subtract each documented deduction: unpaid rent, cleaning, repairs and other charges like unreturned keys.
  3. If money is left, that's the refund due. If the deductions are bigger than the deposit, the difference is a balance the tenant still owes.

Normal wear and tear (faded paint, light carpet wear from walking, small nail holes in many places) generally can't be charged to the deposit. Damage can: a hole in the wall, pet stains, broken blinds, or a unit left dirty. Deadlines to return the deposit, interest rules and what counts as a valid deduction vary by state and city, so check your local rules before sending the statement. Worked example: a typical move-out

The deposit is $1,800, rent is paid in full, and the walkthrough turns up three documented charges:

Carpet cleaning for pet stains (beyond normal wear)$150.00
Patch and paint a hole in the bedroom wall$325.00
Two unreturned keys and a lock rekey$40.00
Total deductions$515.00
Refund due: $1,800 minus $515$1,285.00

The landlord keeps about 28.6% of the deposit and mails $1,285 with the itemized list. Now suppose the tenant also left owing $1,950 in rent. Deductions become $2,465, which is $665 more than the deposit, so the refund is $0 and the tenant still owes $665. If the deposit earned interest at 1% a year for 12 months, the tenant is credited $18 more: a $1,303 refund in the first case.

Keep the paper trail

Deposit disputes are usually won by whoever has the records: a dated move-in checklist with photos, the move-out photos, receipts or contractor estimates for each charge, and the date the refund and statement were sent. Record the deposit as its own line when it comes in, not as rent income, and record each deduction as it's applied. FAQ

How do I calculate my security deposit refund?

Add any interest owed to the deposit, then subtract every documented deduction: unpaid rent and fees, cleaning beyond normal wear, damage repairs and other charges. What's left is the refund. For a $1,800 deposit with $515 in deductions, the refund is $1,285.

What can a landlord deduct from a security deposit?

Generally unpaid rent and lease fees, cleaning needed to return the unit to move-in condition, and repairs for damage beyond normal wear and tear, such as holes in walls, pet damage or broken fixtures. Exact rules vary by state and city.

Can a landlord charge for normal wear and tear?

Generally no. Normal aging from everyday use, like light carpet wear, minor scuffs and faded paint, is the landlord's cost. Damage caused by the tenant, a pet or a guest can be deducted.

What if the damage costs more than the deposit?

The tenant still owes the difference. The calculator shows it as the balance owed. Landlords usually send the itemized list with a bill for the balance, and may pursue it in small claims court if it isn't paid.

Do landlords have to pay interest on security deposits?

Only in some states and cities, and the rates and rules differ. If yours requires it, enter the annual rate and the months held. The calculator uses simple interest, which is how most of those rules work; check your local rule for the exact method.

How long does a landlord have to return a security deposit?

It depends on the state and sometimes the city. Many set deadlines somewhere around two weeks to a month after move-out, and many require an itemized statement of deductions. Look up your local deadline before move-out day.
